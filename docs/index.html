Method and validation

The power balance

A plasma is heated by its own fusion reactions and by external power. It loses energy as radiation and by heat leaking out. In steady state the two sides are equal:

Solving for the product of density and confinement time gives the Lawson criterion used in the explorer, here in the form that allows an impure plasma:

The reactor designer adds real geometry and profiles. It predicts the confinement time from the machine itself with the empirical law fitted to tokamak experiments worldwide:

Check 2: the SPARC design

Every input is taken from the SPARC design paper (Creely et al., 2020). Nothing was adjusted.

Check 3: ITER, and an honest caveat

Predictions are very sensitive to how peaked the temperature profile is assumed to be. ITER's design gain is reproduced with a broad profile; a peaked one nearly doubles it.

What the model leaves out

  • The explorer treats the plasma as uniform, with equal ion and electron temperatures. Fed with peak values it is optimistic, by up to 3.5 times for the real shots examined in the report.
  • The designer assumes simple profile shapes, standard H-mode confinement and no fast-particle losses. It does not model the plasma edge, the exhaust, the magnets or the wall.
  • Radiation is bremsstrahlung, optionally scaled up to stand in for impurity line radiation. Synchrotron radiation is neglected.
  • Limits shown are the standard empirical ones. Staying inside them is necessary, not sufficient.
  • Professional design codes such as PROCESS and cfspopcon go much further. This tool is for learning and first estimates.
